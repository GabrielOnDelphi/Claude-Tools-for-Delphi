# Topic: factual inventory

Load this when a video teaches **no transferable technique** — so both the code and AI topic files correctly fail — but its subject is a decision Gabriel is actively researching outside software: a medical question, a treatment, a purchase, a legal or financial choice. A first-person patient account or a product owner's report carries nothing to adapt, yet the *specifics* are the whole value, and a bare short-version summary throws them away.

Section heading to use: `## What the speaker actually reported`

The four parts below are the point. This section legitimately exceeds the 250-word topic-section budget — do not compress it to fit.

## 1. Every concrete item, quoted verbatim

Every intervention, recommendation, product, procedure or step the speaker credits — **in their own words, inside quotation marks**. Never paraphrase into a characterisation. An adjective of yours replacing a fact of theirs later carries an argument to a doctor, a seller or a lawyer, and it will not survive contact.

## 2. Every number — and every number that is missing

Doses, durations, dates, prices, measurements, model numbers, test results. Then say explicitly what was **never given**: "no drug doses at all", "no heart-rate figures", "price mentioned once, currency never stated". The missing numbers are usually what decides whether the account is usable.

## 3. What did NOT work

Everything the speaker says failed, backfired, or made things worse — stated as plainly as the successes. This half is routinely dropped from summaries and is often the more useful half.

## 4. How they know what they claim to know

Diagnostic route, who tested what, over how long, self-reported or measured, and what is never stated. One person's outcome is n=1: say so once, plainly, without lecturing.

## Labelling and the fact-check pairing

Everything here is **personal experience, labelled as such** — never restated as fact. Pair this section with the `## Claims worth verifying` section and make it do real work: say explicitly where the speaker **aligns with** mainstream literature and where they **conflict with** it, with the URL. The conflicts are the highest-value lines in the whole summary.

## The honest-misfit rule

This topic file is for a decision he is actually making. It is **not** a licence to reinstate a topic section for ordinary news, industry drama, business or political analysis — those still get no section at all, silently. If you cannot name the decision the video feeds, the gate has failed and you write nothing.
